PHONE LINK: WIFI OR BLUETOOTH (BLE)

WHAT THIS IS ABOUT
The radio talks to other radios over LoRa: miles, no network,
no phone. WiFi and Bluetooth are only for the last few metres:
radio to a phone or PC next to you. Mostly that means ATAK (the
team map app, see Help-14-ATAK), and a few WiFi-only extras.
You never need either one to send messages between radios.

THE SHORT ANSWER
- No phone with you, or only messaging: use NEITHER. Longest
  battery. Eco or Saver mode turn WiFi off for you.
- ATAK on an Android phone, out in the field: BLUETOOTH.
- ATAK on iPhone (iTAK), Windows (WinTAK), several phones at
  once, or at home / base camp with WiFi: WIFI.
- Home Assistant voice, TCP hub, mesh over WiFi: WIFI only.

WIFI
How it works: the radio joins a WiFi network (your router or a
phone's hotspot). ATAK on any device on that network finds the
radio by itself (multicast 239.2.3.1 port 6969).
Good:
- Nothing to install: stock ATAK, iTAK and WinTAK just see it.
- Many phones and PCs at once.
- Range of the WiFi network (a whole house or camp).
- Also carries Home Assistant, the TCP hub, mesh over WiFi.
Not so good:
- Needs a network. In the field that means a phone hotspot,
  which drains the phone too.
- Not encrypted: anyone on that network can read positions and
  could send fake ones. Use your own network or hotspot only.
- Guest networks and PCs set to "Public" often block it.
Set up:
1 Radio: M, S, 3 (WiFi), 3 Scan networks, pick one, password.
2 Radio: row 9 ATAK feed ON, row 10 link = WiFi (the default).
3 Phone: join the same network, open ATAK. Done: the radio and
  its contacts appear within 30 s.

BLUETOOTH (BLE, BLUETOOTH LOW ENERGY)
How it works: the radio and ONE phone pair directly, no network
at all. A small app on the phone, "RatSpeak ATAK bridge", passes
the data to ATAK on the same phone. BLE is the low-power kind of
Bluetooth (the kind watches use); this radio has no other kind.
Good:
- No network, no hotspot, nothing else to carry: best in the
  field.
- Paired with a PIN and encrypted: only your phone gets the data.
- Phone reconnects by itself when it comes back in range.
Not so good:
- Android only for now (no iPhone app yet).
- One phone per radio.
- About 10 m: keep the phone on you.
- While the feed uses Bluetooth, the radio's WiFi stays off
  (both do not fit in its memory at once).
Set up, radio:
1 M, S, 3 (WiFi): row 9 ATAK feed ON.
2 Row 10 "ATAK link to phone": Enter -> Bluetooth. The first
  time the radio restarts once.
3 Row 11 shows the radio's name and PIN:
  "RatSpeak-xxxx PIN 123456".
Set up, phone (once):
1 Copy RatSpeak-ATAK-bridge.apk to the phone (from the firmware
  folder of the RatSpeak files, by USB cable, email or a cloud
  drive). Tap it to install; Android asks to allow installs
  from that app: allow it once.
2 Open the bridge app. Allow "Nearby devices" (Bluetooth) and
  notifications. (Android 8-11 asks for location instead: that
  is how old Android names Bluetooth scanning.)
3 Tap Scan for radios, tap RatSpeak-xxxx.
4 Android asks for a pairing PIN: type the 6 digits from row 11
  (they also pop up on the radio's screen for 30 s).
5 The app says "connected to RatSpeak-xxxx". Leave it running;
  a small notification shows it is working.
Set up, ATAK (once, for your markers to reach the radio):
- ATAK, Settings, Network Preferences, Manage Outputs, add:
  address 127.0.0.1, port 4349, protocol UDP.
- Nothing else: ATAK already listens where the app sends
  (127.0.0.1 port 4242). Your own ATAK position reaches the
  radio without any setting.
Forget a phone: radio row 11, Enter twice; on the phone, forget
"RatSpeak-xxxx" in Android's Bluetooth settings.

ENERGY: WHAT IT COSTS
The radio saves battery by sleeping between jobs. Joined WiFi,
or Bluetooth on, keeps it awake: it cannot sleep in any mode.
- Measured 23-24 Nov 2026: Balanced with WiFi joined and the
  ATAK feed on drew ~90 mA and ran 12.6 and 13.5 hours on a
  full battery (two radios).
- Bluetooth: the radio is awake the same way; the Bluetooth part
  itself is small. Expect about the same, 13-15 hours (not
  measured yet).
- Neither on: the radio sleeps; Eco and Saver last several days.
- WiFi allowed but not joined (no saved network in reach): the
  WiFi radio is off; every 3 minutes it looks for a few seconds.
  That costs little (about 1-2 mA on average), the radio sleeps.
  Only a JOINED network keeps it awake.
The Power page (M, S, 5) shows it: "ON ~19 h, awake: Bluetooth"
and row Now "kept awake by WiFi".
Tips:
- Turn the feed off when nobody looks at ATAK: S, 3, 9.
- WiFi joined but not needed? S, 3, 5 Disconnect.
- On a charger it does not matter: the radio runs Full anyway.
- The phone side: the bridge app costs the phone little; a WiFi
  hotspot on the phone costs it a lot.

WHEN NOT TO USE EITHER
- Long trips on battery with no ATAK user: leave both off, pick
  Eco, Saver or Check-in.
- Privacy: with both off the radio sends nothing but LoRa
  (end-to-end encrypted messages).
- Crowded WiFi or no hotspot: Bluetooth or nothing.

TROUBLE?
- Phone app never finds the radio: radio row 11 must show a PIN
  (not "off in this energy mode": Eco, Saver, Check-in turn
  phone links off; pick Balanced or Full). Bluetooth on in the
  phone. Within a few metres.
- Pairing fails again and again: forget on both sides (above),
  scan and pair again.
- App "connected" but ATAK shows nothing: wait 30 s (one round);
  in the app "to ATAK" should count up. Check ATAK has not
  turned its default inputs off.
- WiFi link, ATAK shows nothing: same network? Hotspot or home
  WiFi, not a guest network.

REMOTE CONSOLE (for testing)
Settings > WiFi > Remote console sends the radio's test
console over WiFi to a relay on the home network, so the
radio can be checked without a USB cable. Off unless you
turn it on. Needs the relay's address and key (the same key
on every radio). Uses more battery while on.
