HELP: SHARE YOUR LOCATION

In a chat, MIC opens the location menu (MIC or ^ closes it):

1 Share my location: sends your position once. Without a fresh GPS fix it sends the last fix and its age.
2 Auto-share location: Enter steps off, every 5, 15, 30, 60 min.
3 Auto-share for: 1 h, 4 h or until off.
4 Stop all auto-sharing.
5 Show them on the map.

While auto-share is on, the chat header shows [loc 15m] and the top bar a small pin.

A position you receive shows as [loc]. Enter on the empty message line opens it on the map.

Arrival alerts: M, S, 9 (Alerts), 5. "Tell me when Alice is within 500 m of me (or of a waypoint)": checked each time her position arrives; a beep and a message. Once, or every time she comes back.

Range test: M, S, 8 (LoRa), 6, pick the other radio (it does the same). Both ping each other and log signal strength and GPS position to the SD card (ratdeckpro/rangetest). The screen shows pings, replies and lost ones; 1-4 sets 10/15/30/60 s, ^ stops. On a PC, tools/rangetest/rangetest_map.py turns the file into a map for Google Earth.

MARKERS (items of interest)

On the map, J lists the markers, nearest first, with distance and direction. Alt+J makes one at the map cross.
The form: 1 type (hazard, person, vehicle, camp, water, meet-up, other), 2 at the map cross or from my position, 3 distance, 4 direction (N, NE, E...), 5 a short note, 6 save.
A marker: 1 shows it on the map (a triangle, with a line from you), 2 sends it to a contact or a group, 3 twice removes it.
A marker you receive is kept and drawn on the map. The chat shows "[marker] hazard 640 m NE of you: bridge out", measured from where you are. Enter on the empty message line opens it on the map.
In the marker list, J shows the contacts who shared a position, nearest first. Enter shows one on the map.
Other apps see the marker as text: "Marker: hazard at 43.64012, -70.92011 (bridge out)".

ATAK: phones running ATAK on the same WiFi can see this unit, your contacts' shared positions and the markers. M, S, 3 (WiFi), last row: ATAK feed on. It sends every 30 s to ATAK's usual address (239.2.3.1:6969), no server needed. It also listens there: ATAK phones show on the map as squares, and their markers join your marker list (from ATAK).

GPS needs open sky. Indoors it often gets no fix.

How good is the fix: good = within about 30 m (100 ft), fair = up to 300 m (1000 ft), rough = worse, up to about a mile. The level and the estimated error ("~12 m") show in M, S, 2, row 3 and on the GPS signals page; the map footer shows the error after the coordinates, or "rough". A rough fix is shown and can be shared, but it does not go into the track. "TIME ONLY" on the GPS signals page: the receiver has the time (the clock is set from it) but no position yet.

Before a new fix comes, the radio uses the last one and says how old it is ("fix 3h old"). It keeps the last position when switched off or restarted. It also keeps the receiver's satellite data and hands it back, with the time and the last position, each time the receiver is switched on, so the next fix comes sooner.

Watch the satellites: M, S, 1 0 (Diagnostics), 1 0 (GPS signals). A bar for every satellite the receiver hears, taller = stronger, with the satellite's number under it. It redraws every 2 seconds and the radio stays awake while it shows. A first fix needs about four bars above the dotted line (25) for a minute or two. Few or short bars: move to open sky, away from metal and other electronics. The top line says FIX or NO FIX, for how long, and whether the radio runs on USB or battery.
