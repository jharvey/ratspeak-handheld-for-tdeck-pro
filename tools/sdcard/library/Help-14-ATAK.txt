ATAK FEED (CURSOR ON TARGET)

WHAT ATAK IS
ATAK (Android Team Awareness Kit) is a free map app used by search
and rescue, fire crews, police and the military. iPhone has iTAK,
Windows has WinTAK. Each shows the team on a map: who is where, and
markers such as hazards or meeting points. The apps talk in "Cursor
on Target" (CoT): short XML messages with a position, a name and a
type.

WHAT THIS RADIO DOES WITH IT
- Sends: itself (name, position, battery), the last shared position
  of each contact, and your markers. ATAK shows the radio and its
  contacts as friendly units, markers as map points.
- Receives: ATAK users show on the Atlas as squares, with their
  name. Their markers become markers here (triangles), "from ATAK".
- A bridge: contacts that reach you only over LoRa (no phone, no
  WiFi, miles away) still show up in ATAK through this radio.

TURN IT ON
M, S, 3 (WiFi), row "ATAK feed (CoT)", Enter = on / off.
Off by default. It sends every 30 s.
Next row "ATAK link to phone": Enter switches WiFi / Bluetooth
(the first switch to Bluetooth restarts the radio once).

HOW IT REACHES THE PHONE: WIFI OR BLUETOOTH
LoRa carries messages, positions and markers between radios, miles
apart. Only the last few metres, radio to phone, need WiFi or
Bluetooth.
- WiFi: radio and phone on the same WiFi network (a phone hotspot
  works). Sent to ATAK's standard address 239.2.3.1 port 6969, so
  ATAK finds it with no setup. Nothing to install on the phone.
- Bluetooth: no network needed at all, good in the field. Needs the
  free "RatSpeak ATAK bridge" app on the phone (Android). While the
  feed uses Bluetooth, the radio's WiFi stays off (both do not fit
  in memory at once).
- Neither is turned on by itself. In an energy mode with WiFi off
  (Saver, Check-in) the feed waits, Bluetooth too.
- Off, the feed costs no power. On, either link keeps the radio
  awake (no sleep): plan for a day of battery, not several.

BLUETOOTH: PAIR THE PHONE (ONCE)
1 Radio: feed ON, link Bluetooth. The row below shows the radio's
  name (RatSpeak-xxxx) and a 6-digit code.
2 Phone: install the bridge app (RatSpeak-ATAK-bridge.apk), open it,
  allow Bluetooth / Nearby devices, tap Scan, tap the radio.
3 Android asks for a pairing code: type the code the radio shows
  (it also pops up on the radio's screen).
4 The app shows "connected"; ATAK shows the radio and its contacts.
  After that the phone reconnects by itself.
- Your own markers to the radio: in ATAK add an output once
  (Settings, Network Preferences, Manage Outputs: 127.0.0.1, port
  4349, UDP). Your ATAK position reaches the radio without it.
- Forget the paired phones: the Bluetooth row, Enter twice.
- Only a phone that typed the code gets data: the link is encrypted.

STRENGTHS
- No TAK server, no internet, no account: same WiFi, or Bluetooth
  with no network at all.
- LoRa radios with no phone join the ATAK picture.
- ATAK users' positions and markers reach the Atlas; a marker from
  ATAK can be sent on to your LoRa contacts like any other.
- Small: one short message per item every 30 s.

WEAKNESSES
- Radio to phone is short range: WiFi tens of metres, Bluetooth
  about 10 m (keep the phone on you).
- WiFi is not encrypted: anyone on the same WiFi can see the
  positions, and can send fake ones. Use your own network or
  hotspot. Bluetooth is encrypted and paired.
- Bluetooth needs the bridge app (Android only for now; iTAK on
  iPhone and WinTAK use WiFi).
- Positions of contacts are only as fresh as their last share.
- Limits: 16 ATAK users kept (oldest dropped), at most 12 of the 24
  markers come from ATAK, so your contacts' markers are never pushed
  out. Markers from ATAK are not sent back to ATAK.
- Some networks (guest WiFi, a PC network set to Public) block the
  multicast address; then nothing arrives.

GOOD TO KNOW
- Tested 23 Nov 2026: a PC on the WiFi received every event as valid
  CoT (units and markers). Bluetooth: tested in the simulator and
  the radio side on a T-Deck; not yet tried with a real ATAK phone.
- Hostile units (red, a-h) from ATAK show as hazard markers.
- Markers: Help-6-Map, Atlas J. Sharing your position: Help-5.
