ATAK FEED (CURSOR ON TARGET)

WHAT ATAK IS
ATAK (Android Team Awareness Kit) is a free map app used by search
and rescue, fire crews, police and the military. iPhone has iTAK,
Windows has WinTAK. Each shows the team on a map: who is where, and
markers such as hazards or meeting points. The apps talk in "Cursor
on Target" (CoT): short XML messages with a position, a name and a
type.

WHAT THIS RADIO DOES WITH IT
- Sends: itself (name, position, battery), the last shared position
  of each contact, and your markers. ATAK shows the radio and its
  contacts as friendly units, markers as map points.
- Receives: ATAK users show on the Atlas as squares, with their
  name. Their markers become markers here (triangles), "from ATAK".
- A bridge: contacts that reach you only over LoRa (no phone, no
  WiFi, miles away) still show up in ATAK through this radio.

TURN IT ON
M, S, 3 (WiFi), last row "ATAK feed (CoT)", Enter = on / off.
Off by default. It sends every 30 s, to ATAK's standard address
239.2.3.1 port 6969, so ATAK finds it with no setup and no server.

DOES IT NEED WIFI?
Yes. The feed only runs on WiFi: the radio and the phone must be on
the same WiFi network (a phone hotspot works: join the radio to the
phone's hotspot). LoRa carries the messages, positions and markers
between radios; WiFi carries them to and from ATAK.
- It never turns WiFi on by itself. In an energy mode with WiFi off
  (Saver, Check-in) the feed waits.
- Off, it costs no power. On, the cost is WiFi itself.

STRENGTHS
- No TAK server, no internet, no account: same WiFi is enough.
- LoRa radios with no phone join the ATAK picture.
- ATAK users' positions and markers reach the Atlas; a marker from
  ATAK can be sent on to your LoRa contacts like any other.
- Small: one short message per item every 30 s.

WEAKNESSES
- WiFi range only between radio and phone: tens of metres, not the
  miles LoRa reaches.
- Not encrypted: anyone on the same WiFi can see the positions, and
  can send fake ones. Use it on your own network or hotspot only.
- Positions of contacts are only as fresh as their last share.
- Limits: 16 ATAK users kept (oldest dropped), at most 12 of the 24
  markers come from ATAK, so your contacts' markers are never pushed
  out. Markers from ATAK are not sent back to ATAK.
- Some networks (guest WiFi, a PC network set to Public) block the
  multicast address; then nothing arrives.

GOOD TO KNOW
- Tested 23 Nov 2026: a PC on the WiFi received every event as valid
  CoT (units and markers). Not yet tried with a real ATAK phone.
- Hostile units (red, a-h) from ATAK show as hazard markers.
- Markers: Help-6-Map, Atlas J. Sharing your position: Help-5.
